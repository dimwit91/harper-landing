What she does all day for the person who built her.

Harper started as one person’s assistant on his work email, his calendar, his texts and his phone. This is what she runs for him now, most of it on a schedule.

  1. Two texts a day about what matters.

    At 8am on weekdays she texts him the day’s priorities, pulled from his calendar and inbox. At 1pm she checks what’s still open.

  2. The inbox gets swept every hour.

    Weekday triage runs hourly, and marketing mail gets an unsubscribe sweep once a day. Card due-date reminders are archived. Only a late-payment email reaches him.

  3. Scheduling replies go out with real times.

    She offers a few open slots that skip travel, family and focus blocks, shows both time zones, and books it when they reply. Mondays get a commute block, and big meetings get a prep block in front of them.

  4. Some email goes out on its own clock.

    Every Sunday she sends him a report of what the team of bots bought that week. Student check-in emails go out about a day before each session.

  5. She keeps watch so he doesn’t have to.

    She checks for invites nobody answered, brings back snoozed mail when it’s due, and runs a used-car search that only pings him on a real match. New applicant posts in Slack trigger a text to the applicant.

  6. Texts follow house rules.

    She only texts between 5:00am and 10:30pm. Before she retries a send she checks whether the first one went through, so nobody gets the same text twice.

  7. She works with other bots.

    A chief of staff bot and an ops bot hand her texts and leads to send. She sends them as Harper and reports back.

  8. Calls run through a voice agent.

    When a job needs a phone call, she makes it with a voice agent and tells him how it went.

Yours in a sitting

Install Harper. Then tell her how you work.

She ships with strong defaults. After install she asks one playbook question at a time, and saves the answer.

  1. Name

    Keep Harper, or rename. Title stays Assistant. The rule does not change: she is not you.

  2. Mailbox

    Connect the work inbox she should send from. Optionally connect a personal mailbox for spam sweep and booking receipts.

  3. Calendar rules

    Name the work calendar versus any shared calendar. Spell out protected blocks. She will cluster people-meetings and leave holes.

  4. Perfect Week

    She does not copy someone else’s calendar. She asks how you want the week to run: no-meeting windows, people-days vs deep-work days, default meeting length, same-day requests. Then she iterates. What worked. What did not. What to adjust next week. One question at a time.

  5. Draft vs send

    Out of the box: scheduling may send; everything else waits. Lock more classes when you trust them.

  6. Her own line

    Give her one dedicated assistant line. Not your cell. With a Mac mini running BlueBubbles she gets full iMessage, and plain SMS works as a fallback. Text her from your phone, or add her to a group. The first text in a new thread says who she is. Later texts don’t sign off.

  7. Voice

    Calls run through a voice agent. She answers as Harper. Outbound calling stays off until you say it’s live.

Before you add her

Questions.

Does Harper pretend to be me?

No. She is Harper. External people hear that on the first message. Later replies still sign Harper. She will match your phrasing. She will not put your name on a message she wrote as if you typed it.

Do I have to be in Grok Bot to use her?

No. Text her line from your phone, or add her to a group. She sees new texts right away and replies as Harper on that thread.

What can she send without asking me?

Scheduling and reschedules, so booking does not stall. You can lock more send-classes later. Everything else is a draft until you say yes.

Will she flood my chat?

No. She pings when something was sent, booked, forwarded, or needs a decision. Afternoon priority checks stay quiet unless the day actually changed.

Can she use my personal cell?

Give her her own number. You text that number from your phone. Do not run Harper on your cell. Mixing her line with yours, or with another specialist bot, is how identity breaks. Inbound voice, when you turn it on, is on that same assistant number.

What happens to mail I actually have to do?

It does not sit in Inbox as a guilt pile. She puts it on that day’s single Email action block, a numbered list with links, then files the thread. You work the calendar, not the unread count.

A few more

What about a family or shared calendar?

She uses it only when a partner needs to be involved. Your work and solo personal items stay on your calendar so the shared one does not become a junk drawer.

How does she know what is junk vs. a real person?

The mailbox, not a CRM. If you have written them, they are internal, or you are in a real back-and-forth, they are known. One-way pitches stay filed.

Can I add my own jobs later?

Yes. Recipes you add after install. They are not in the public default, because they are yours, not everyone’s.

Does she place outbound calls?

Not until you turn that on. Inbound voice answers as Harper. Outbound calling is off by default.

What if I ask her to do something outside email, calendar, or her number?

She hands it back to your lead agent. That is the lane. That is how she stays good.

What about verification codes?

She never copies, reports, or uses OTP, 2FA, or bank codes that land on email or SMS. Those are yours.
